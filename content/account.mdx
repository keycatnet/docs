# Your account and security

## Who controls the money

Each business you create on Keycat has its own account on Base: a
[Safe](https://safe.global) smart account. Your **passkey** owns it, meaning the
device key you sign in with. Money leaves the account only when an owner signs:

- **Withdrawals** (**Send money out** in the dashboard) to the one withdrawal
  address saved under **Settings → Where you send money**.
- **Refunds**, which send the refund straight to the address the customer
  paid from. See [Refunds](/refunds).

Keycat holds no key that can move your money. Each of those operations pays
its network fee in USDC from your account, so you never need ETH. "Send all"
sends everything except that operation's exact network fee reserve, which the
review shows before you sign. The details are in
[The business account](/protocol/account) and
[Operations and network fees](/protocol/operations).

## Your Keycat name

The handle you choose for a business is also its name: `joescoffee` gives a
payment page at `pay.keycat.net/joescoffee` and the ENS name
`joescoffee.keycat.eth`. Wallets that support ENS resolve the name to your
business account's address, along with its name, description, picture, and
website from your profile.

Payments that should be tracked go through a charge, a payment link, or your
payment page: those are what produce receipts and webhooks.

## Payout groups

A payout group shares payments with other Keycat businesses, each by a fixed
share; your business keeps the rest. Create one from your business's Home
under **Payout groups**, then pick it when you create a payment link under
**Request**, or
pass its id as `payout_group` when you create a charge through the API. A link
without a group pays your business only. You can also set a group for your
payment page, which covers the payments visitors start there.

Creating a group takes one confirmation with your passkey and a few cents from
your balance, because your own account sets up the group's split. The split
has no owner, so neither you nor Keycat can change its shares or take from it.
Each payment reaches it first, and the split pays every payee and your
business their shares, usually a few seconds later and within a minute at
most. Until then your share shows beside your balance as on its way. See
[Payout groups](/protocol/payouts) for the contract.

A group never changes. To pay different people, create a new group and pick it
for your links. Archiving a group hides it from new links; links that already
use it keep sharing their payments. Refunding a payment made through a group
comes from your balance, because its payees have already been paid.

## Signing in

You sign in at [app.keycat.net](https://app.keycat.net) with a passkey: Face ID,
Touch ID, Windows Hello, or a security key. There is no password, and no
password reset to attack.

## More devices and co-owners

One sign-in account can hold several businesses, and several passkeys.

| You add | What it can do |
| --- | --- |
| A passkey on another device | View every business on the account. It cannot move money until approved. |
| A **backup device** for a business | Sign for that business on its own, so losing one device doesn't lock you out. |
| A **co-owner** for a business | Sign together with you. The business then needs more than one signature to move money. |

Approving a device or co-owner is itself an owner-signed operation, done per
business under **Settings → Who can approve money**. Add a backup device early:
a passkey that existed only on a lost device cannot be recovered by anyone.
See [Passkeys and owners](/protocol/passkeys).

## API keys

An API key belongs to one business. It can:

- create, read, list, and cancel charges,
- read and replay events,
- set the webhook endpoint,
- update the public profile.

It can preview a refund ([`POST /v1/refunds`](/api#refunds)), but it cannot
move money, complete a refund, or change who owns the account. Treat it
as a secret anyway: anyone holding it can create charges in your name and see
your sales.

Keys are created under **Settings → Integrate**. Creating a new key does not
disable older ones, and keys cannot yet be revoked from the dashboard. Create a
new key only when you need one.

## Webhook secrets

The webhook signing secret is shown once, when you set the endpoint. Keycat
stores it encrypted. Setting the endpoint again issues a new secret and retires
the old one immediately.
