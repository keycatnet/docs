# Passkeys and owners

A passkey is a WebAuthn credential: a P-256 key pair whose private key never
leaves your device's secure hardware or your platform's passkey manager.
Keycat stores only the public key. Signing in and approving money both use the
same passkey, in two deliberately different ceremonies.

## The signer contract

A Safe owner must be an address, and a P-256 key has no Ethereum address. Safe's
passkey module bridges the two: `SafeWebAuthnSignerFactory` v0.2.1 deploys a
small proxy, `SafeWebAuthnSignerProxy`, that holds your public key and answers
ERC-1271 `isValidSignature` by checking a WebAuthn assertion against it.

The proxy's address is deterministic:

```text
initCode = proxyCreationCode ‖ uint256(SafeWebAuthnSignerSingleton) ‖ x ‖ y ‖ uint256(verifiers)
signer   = CREATE2(SafeWebAuthnSignerFactory, bytes32(0), keccak256(initCode))
```

`x` and `y` are your public key's coordinates. `verifiers` is a `uint176`
that packs a precompile address in its top 16 bits and a fallback verifier
contract in the low 160. Keycat uses Base's P-256 precompile (RIP-7212, at
address `0x100`) and no fallback, so a signature check costs a few thousand
gas instead of the hundreds of thousands a Solidity P-256 verifier needs. The
factory's own `getSigner(x, y, verifiers)` returns the same address, which is
how you can check it.

The signer contract is published when it is first needed: when the first operation that
needs its signature is about to be signed, Keycat calls the factory's
permissionless `createSigner`. Anyone could make that call, and the result
would be the same contract at the same address.

## Signing in

Sign-in is a discoverable WebAuthn authentication on the relying party
`keycat.net`, from `https://app.keycat.net`, with an empty `allowCredentials`
list, so your device offers whichever Keycat passkeys it holds. Keycat
requires user verification (Face ID, a fingerprint, a device PIN), checks the
origin, the relying party, the challenge and the signature counter, and then
issues a session. A session lets you see your businesses and prepare
operations. It cannot move money by itself.

## Approving money

Every operation that moves money is approved by a separate, one-use ceremony:

- Its challenge is the exact digest being authorised: the `SafeOp` hash that
  Safe4337Module computes (EIP-712, domain bound to chain 8453 and the module),
  or the USDC permit digest for a business's first operation.
- Its `allowCredentials` holds exactly one credential, the owner Keycat
  expects to sign next.
- It is single use, and Keycat checks the credential, origin, relying party,
  challenge, counter, and that the credential is an active owner of that
  business before the signature goes anywhere.

Because the challenge is the operation's hash, the signature commits to every
field of the operation: target, calldata, value, nonce, gas limits, fee caps
and the paymaster data. Nothing can be changed between your review and the
submission without invalidating it.

### What goes on chain

The authenticator returns `authenticatorData`, `clientDataJSON` and a DER
signature. Keycat decodes the DER signature strictly into `r` and `s`, and
splits `clientDataJSON` into the part after the challenge
(`clientDataFields`). The signer contract rebuilds the JSON from the
operation hash, so the challenge is never trusted from the client:

```text
clientDataJSON = '{"type":"webauthn.get","challenge":"' ‖ base64url(hash) ‖ '",' ‖ clientDataFields ‖ '}'
message        = sha256(authenticatorData ‖ sha256(clientDataJSON))
valid          = P256.verify(message, r, s, x, y)
```

Each owner's signature is wrapped as a Safe contract signature (`v = 0`, `r`
the signer contract's address, `s` the offset of
`abi.encode(authenticatorData, clientDataFields, r, s)` in the dynamic part),
and the signatures are sorted by signer address as Safe requires.

## Backup devices and co-owners

One Keycat sign-in account can hold several passkeys and several businesses.
Authority is recorded per passkey, per business:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| View only | The passkey can sign in and see the business. It is not an owner of the Safe. |
| Pending | An owner has signed the operation that adds it, and it is on its way. |
| Active | Its signer contract is an owner of the Safe. |

A new passkey starts as view only. An existing owner turns it into an owner by
signing an operation that calls `addOwnerWithThreshold(signer, threshold)` on
each business they choose. Keycat publishes the new passkey's signer contract
first, so the Safe gains an owner with code behind it.

- A **backup device** keeps the threshold at 1. Either passkey can approve
  money on its own, so losing one device does not lock you out.
- A **co-owner** raises the threshold. Every operation then needs that many
  owner signatures, collected one ceremony at a time; a half-signed operation
  can be continued from another device. Keycat asks for a plain-language
  confirmation before raising a threshold, because a threshold that the
  remaining passkeys cannot meet locks the account for good.

Keycat calls an account backed up only when every business it owns has at
least two active owners.

## Losing a passkey

Passkeys synced by your platform (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager,
and similar) survive the loss of one device. A passkey that lived only on a
lost device, with no backup owner, cannot be recovered by Keycat or anyone
else: there is no admin key and no reset path, which is the same property that
stops Keycat from moving your money. Add a backup device before the account
holds money you cannot afford to lose.
