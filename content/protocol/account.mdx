# The business account

Every business on Keycat is its own [Safe](https://github.com/safe-global/safe-smart-account)
smart account on Base. It is a standard Safe proxy, created through Safe's own
factory with Safe's own singleton, so any tool that understands Safe can read
it.

| Part | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| Singleton | SafeL2 1.4.1 |
| Proxy factory | SafeProxyFactory 1.4.1, `createProxyWithNonce` |
| ERC-4337 support | Safe4337Module v0.3.0, enabled at creation and set as the fallback handler |
| Module setup | SafeModuleSetup v0.3.0, delegatecalled once during `setup` |
| EntryPoint | ERC-4337 EntryPoint v0.7 |
| First owner | A SafeWebAuthnSigner v0.2.1 proxy for your passkey ([Passkeys](/protocol/passkeys)) |

Addresses for all of them are in [Contracts](/protocol/contracts).

## Why SafeL2

SafeL2 and the plain Safe singleton report the same version and differ only in
the events they emit. SafeL2 emits `SafeMultiSigTransaction` and
`SafeModuleTransaction` on every execution, which is what Safe's Transaction
Service indexes on L2 networks. A treasury on the plain singleton would not
appear in Safe's own interface. The extra cost is roughly 7,000 to 10,000 gas
per execution, a fraction of a cent on Base, and only on money going out:
receiving a payment never executes Safe code.

## The initializer

The Safe is created with this exact `setup` call. Nothing about it changes
after the business is created.

```solidity
Safe.setup(
  _owners:          [signerProxy],          // your passkey's signer contract
  _threshold:       1,
  to:               SafeModuleSetup,
  data:             enableModules([Safe4337Module]),
  fallbackHandler:  Safe4337Module,
  paymentToken:     address(0),
  payment:          0,
  paymentReceiver:  address(0)
)
```

`setup` delegatecalls `SafeModuleSetup.enableModules`, so the 4337 module is
already enabled when the Safe exists, and the first operation can run
through it. Setting the module as the fallback handler alone would not be
enough: `execTransactionFromModule` checks that the caller is an enabled
module.

## A counterfactual address

The address is fixed before the Safe exists. SafeProxyFactory deploys with
CREATE2, so the address is a function of the factory, the singleton, the
initializer above and a salt:

```text
salt    = keccak256(keccak256(initializer) ‖ saltNonce)
address = CREATE2(SafeProxyFactory, salt, keccak256(proxyCreationCode ‖ uint256(SafeL2)))
```

`saltNonce` is derived from Keycat's identifiers for your account and the
business, so two businesses owned by the same passkey get two different
accounts. Because the initializer names your signer contract, whose own
address is derived from your passkey's public key, the account address is
ultimately a function of your passkey.

Keycat computes this derivation twice, independently, when you create a
business, and refuses to create it if the two disagree. A wrong derivation
would produce an address whose owner can never be deployed, which would
freeze every payment sent to it.

## Deployed by its first operation

Nothing is deployed when you create a business. Payments can arrive at the
counterfactual address straight away: USDC is a balance in the USDC contract,
and it does not need code at the recipient.

The Safe is deployed by its first ERC-4337 operation, usually your first
withdrawal, refund or savings change. That operation carries the factory and
`createProxyWithNonce` calldata in its `factory` and `factoryData` fields, so
the EntryPoint deploys the Safe and runs the operation in one transaction. Its
gas, deployment included, is paid from the Safe's own USDC
([Operations and network fees](/protocol/operations)). A business with no
USDC cannot deploy its account, and has nothing to move yet either.

## Using the Safe outside Keycat

Because the account is a standard Safe, its balance, owners, threshold and
modules can be read with any Safe tooling or a block explorer, and its history
is indexed by Safe's Transaction Service.

<Callout type="warning">
  Signing is the part that depends on Keycat. A passkey is bound to the
  relying party `keycat.net`: your device will only produce a signature for a
  page served from `keycat.net`. The signer contract on chain does not check
  the origin, but your authenticator does, so another site cannot ask your
  passkey to sign. That is the protection that stops a phishing page from
  spending your money, and it also means that today the passkey signs only
  through Keycat.
</Callout>

## Which money counts

Your balance is canonical USDC on Base held by the Safe, plus your savings
position in Aave (its aUSDC balance). Keycat reads both directly from Base,
never from its own records. Other tokens sent to the address are held by the
Safe, but Keycat does not show or move them.
