# Operations and network fees

Money leaves a business account only as an ERC-4337 user operation that the
account's owners signed. This page covers what those operations can be, how
they reach the network, and how they pay for gas in USDC.

## The fixed menu

Keycat builds operations from a fixed list. It never accepts calldata, a
destination, an amount, gas values or paymaster data from a browser; it builds
each operation from what it has stored and the action you chose.

| Operation | What the Safe executes |
| --- | --- |
| Send | `USDC.transfer(savedWithdrawalAddress, amount)` |
| Send, topped up from savings | `Pool.withdraw(USDC, shortfall, safe)` then `USDC.transfer(...)`, in one batch |
| Refund | `USDC.transfer(customerOrConversionAddress, amount)`, after a savings withdrawal when one is needed ([Refunds](/refunds)) |
| Turn savings on or off | The Roles modifier deployment, scoping and module changes ([Savings](/protocol/savings)) |
| Make more available | `Pool.withdraw(USDC, amount, safe)` |
| Create a payout group | `PushSplitFactory.createSplitDeterministic(...)` ([Payout groups](/protocol/payouts)) |
| Add an owner | `addOwnerWithThreshold(signer, threshold)` |

A withdrawal goes only to the address saved under **Settings → Where you send
money**.

## How an operation executes

The Safe's 4337 module is both an enabled module and the Safe's fallback
handler. The EntryPoint calls `validateUserOp` on the Safe, which the
fallback handler forwards to the module; the module checks the owners'
signatures over the `SafeOp` hash and pays the EntryPoint what the paymaster
does not cover (here, nothing). Execution then calls the module's
`executeUserOp(to, value, data, operation)`, which calls
`execTransactionFromModule` on the Safe.

- A single call uses `operation = 0` (call).
- Two legs, such as a savings withdrawal followed by a transfer, are one
  `MultiSendCallOnly` 1.4.1 batch that the Safe delegatecalls
  (`operation = 1`). The `CallOnly` variant reverts if anything inside the
  batch tries to delegatecall, so the batch cannot reach back and rewrite the
  Safe's storage. One signature commits to both legs.

Operations use nonce key 0 and run one at a time per business. A second
operation waits until the first is mined or cancelled; this is why the
dashboard sometimes says another approval is still finishing.

Operations are submitted to a public ERC-4337 bundler and followed to their
receipt. Keycat reports an operation as done only from its
`UserOperationEvent` with `success = true` and the real transaction hash.
`handleOps` itself succeeds even when an operation's inner call reverts, so
the transaction's status alone proves nothing.

## Gas in USDC

The business account never holds ETH. Every operation names
[Circle Paymaster](https://developers.circle.com/stablecoins/paymaster-overview)
for EntryPoint v0.7 as its paymaster. Circle fronts the ETH for gas and
charges the Safe in USDC after the operation runs.

`paymasterData` is Circle's layout:

```text
uint8 mode (0) ‖ address token (USDC) ‖ uint256 permitAmount ‖ bytes permitSignature
```

**The first operation** carries a USDC permit (EIP-2612) signed by the Safe:
your passkey signs the permit digest in its own ceremony, the Safe verifies it
through ERC-1271, and Circle uses it to take its fee for that operation. The
same operation also calls `USDC.approve(CirclePaymaster, allowance)`, so every
later operation leaves the permit empty and Circle reads the allowance
instead. The allowance is a fixed multiple of the per-operation cap
(1,000 operations' worth), never `type(uint256).max`. It is a ceiling on what
Circle may pull in total, not a payment: Circle takes only what each operation
used.

## What a network fee costs

Before an operation runs, Circle reserves its worst case from the Safe's
available USDC (the prefund), then refunds what the operation did not use.
The prefund follows Circle's own contract:

```text
base    = (additionalGasCharge + Σ gas limits) × maxFeePerGas × usdcPerEth / 10¹⁸ + 1
prefund = base + base × feeSpread / 10,000
```

`additionalGasCharge`, `feeSpread` and the USDC price of ETH are read from
the paymaster at the moment the operation is built, not assumed. The gas
limits are the operation's own call, verification and pre-verification
limits, plus the paymaster's verification limit (200,000 gas once the Safe has
approved Circle, 800,000 for the permit on the first operation) and its
post-operation limit (100,000).

Every review sheet shows the network fee as "up to" that amount, because it
is the reserved maximum. What Circle actually charged is read afterwards from
the `actualTokenNeeded` field of its `UserOperationSponsored` event, and is
usually much less. On Base at its usual fee level, a send from an existing
account reserves a cent or two.

Two consequences follow from the prefund:

- **Send all** sends your available balance less the exact prefund of that
  operation. What is left behind afterwards is the prefund less the real fee,
  as with any wallet's maximum.
- **Savings cannot pay a prefund.** Circle takes the prefund during
  validation, before a savings withdrawal in the same batch has run, so the
  available balance must cover it. An operation that cannot be covered is
  refused before you sign.

Each operation is also capped: Keycat refuses to prepare one whose worst case
exceeds its per-operation limit, and the gas limits and fee caps you sign
bound what Circle can charge.

## The first operation costs more

An account that does not exist yet budgets for its deployment: the Safe proxy,
module setup, permit and approval all run in its first operation. The review
sheet for a first operation says it also sets the account up.
