# How Keycat works

This section is for developers and advanced users who want to know what
happens underneath: which contracts hold the money, what a passkey actually
signs, what Keycat can and cannot do with your account, and how to check each
claim on chain yourself. The rest of the docs describe what to build; these
pages describe what you are trusting.

Everything here is on Base (chain id 8453) unless a page says otherwise.

## The design in one paragraph

Each business is a [Safe](https://safe.global) smart account (SafeL2 1.4.1)
whose only owner, at creation, is a WebAuthn signer contract bound to the
business owner's passkey. The Safe runs ERC-4337 operations through Safe's
4337 module on EntryPoint v0.7, and pays their gas in its own USDC through
Circle's paymaster, so it never holds ETH. Customers pay through
[Relay](https://relay.link) deposit addresses that deliver exactly the invoice
in USDC to the Safe. Savings lend idle USDC to Aave V3 through a Zodiac Roles
modifier that lets Keycat supply, and nothing else. Payout groups are
ownerless 0xSplits push splits. Keycat writes no contracts of its own: every
contract on the money path was written, audited and deployed by its publisher,
and Keycat pins and verifies their addresses.

## What Keycat can do

- Decide where a new payment is routed. Checkout asks Relay for a quote whose
  recipient is your business account (or your payout group's split), and
  shows the payer that quote. This is the largest thing you trust Keycat
  with: a compromised Keycat could route future payments elsewhere. It cannot
  reach money that has already arrived.
- Prepare an operation for you to sign. Keycat builds every withdrawal,
  refund, savings change and payout group from a fixed menu, and shows it in
  full before your passkey signs. Your passkey signs a hash of the exact
  operation, so a change after you review it invalidates the signature.
- Supply your available USDC to Aave on your behalf, once you turn savings
  on. The permission is scoped on chain to supplying USDC into a position
  owned by your Safe. See [Savings](/protocol/savings).
- Pay out a payout group's split. Distribution is a permissionless call; it
  sends shares to the addresses fixed when the split was created, and nowhere
  else. See [Payout groups](/protocol/payouts).
- Answer for `<handle>.keycat.eth`. Name resolution is signed by Keycat. See
  [Names](/protocol/names).

## What Keycat cannot do

- Move money out of your Safe. No Keycat key is an owner of your Safe, and no
  module Keycat controls can transfer from it. The savings modifier can only
  call Aave's `supply` with your Safe as the beneficiary, and USDC's `approve`
  with the Aave pool as the spender.
- Redeem your savings. Aave `withdraw` is not in the scope Keycat holds;
  only an owner's signature takes money out of Aave.
- Change a payout group. Each split is created with no owner and cannot be
  edited by anyone.
- Spend your USDC on gas beyond a bound. The Safe approves Circle's paymaster
  for a fixed allowance, and Circle charges each operation only what that
  operation used, against a cap you approve when you sign.

## Keycat's own accounts

Keycat operates three on-chain accounts, each an ordinary Safe on the same
stack as yours, with one key and gas paid in its own USDC:

| Account | What it calls | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signer publisher | `SafeWebAuthnSignerFactory.createSigner` | Publishes the signer contract for a passkey before its first signature is needed. The call is permissionless and its result depends only on the passkey's public key. |
| Savings operator | Your Roles modifier, within its scope | Supplies available USDC to Aave on behalf of your Safe. |
| Payouts operator | `PushSplit.distribute` | Pays out a payout group's split. Anyone could make this call. |

None of them holds or needs ETH, and none is an owner of any business
account. The worst a stolen operator key can do is spend that operator's own
gas, publish a signer contract that anyone could publish, move a business's
available USDC into that business's own savings, or pay out a split to the
payees it already names.
