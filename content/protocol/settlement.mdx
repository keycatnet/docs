# Payment routing and settlement

Keycat deploys nothing for payments. Each payment attempt is a
[Relay](https://relay.link) strict deposit address: a one-off address on the
payer's network that Relay watches, and that settles to your business account
on Base when the right amount arrives.

## One attempt

When the payer picks a coin and network, checkout asks Keycat for a quote, and
Keycat asks Relay for this:

```json
{
  "originChainId": 42161,
  "originCurrency": "<the payer's coin>",
  "destinationChainId": 8453,
  "destinationCurrency": "0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913",
  "amount": "24500000",
  "tradeType": "EXACT_OUTPUT",
  "recipient": "<your business account, or your payout group's split>",
  "refundTo": "<where the payer said a return should go>",
  "recoveryAddress": "<Keycat's recovery address on that network>",
  "useDepositAddress": true,
  "strict": true,
  "ttl": 3600,
  "appFees": [{ "recipient": "<Keycat>", "fee": "100" }]
}
```

- **`EXACT_OUTPUT`** of the invoice amount, in USDC base units (six
  decimals). The recipient receives exactly the invoice; every cost is added to
  what the payer sends.
- **`strict`** deposit address. Relay fills only when the deposit covers the
  quote. An amount that falls short is returned instead of being converted
  into a smaller payment, so your business never receives a silent partial.
- **`appFees`** is Keycat's 1% (100 basis points), charged by Relay on the
  input side, with Relay's minimum of $0.025 per transaction. A payment on a
  business's own payment page, where the payer chooses the amount, has no app
  fee.
- **`ttl`** of one hour. Each attempt has its own window. A charge stays open
  while any attempt on it is still open, and a payment that lands within its
  attempt's window pays the charge even if the charge has expired since.

The recipient is read from the business's own record when the attempt is
created, never from the browser. Payments through a payout group go to that
group's split address, which is fixed for the group's lifetime.

### The amount to send

Relay quotes the input with every decimal of the payer's coin, and many
wallets cut what you paste to eight places, which lands short of a strict
address. Keycat rounds the input **up** to the fewest decimals at which the
last digit is worth at most one cent (from Relay's own dollar value for the
input; never more than eight places or the coin's own precision). ETH at about
$1,800 is shown to six places, USDC to two. The surplus, under a cent, is
Relay's to refund or keep. Checkout shows and copies that one string verbatim.

### Routes

Checkout lists the coins and networks Relay can route right now, limited to EVM
networks and Bitcoin, with dollar coins first. Each network needs a Keycat
recovery address of its own, and a network without one is not offered. A
quote whose routing fee is more than a tenth of the payment is refused
(`route_too_costly`), which for small payments usually means Ethereum
mainnet. Solana, Tron, TON, XRP and others are not offered.

## Where a return goes

Before checkout shows a deposit address it asks where the payer is paying
from, and that answer becomes the quote's `refundTo`:

| Payer chose | `refundTo` |
| --- | --- |
| A wallet app | Relay's original-sender value for that network, so a return goes to the address that sent the deposit. |
| An exchange account | The address the payer typed, which they own at that exchange. Exchanges send from shared wallets, so a return to the sender would not reach them. |
| Card | Your business account (see [By card](#by-card)). |

A typed address is checked in the browser and again by the API: EIP-55
checksums for mixed-case EVM addresses, bech32 and bech32m or base58check for
Bitcoin. The API refuses your business's own addresses, Keycat's, and the
attempt's own deposit address.

`recoveryAddress` is separate: it is where Relay sends a deposit it can
neither fill nor return. It is a Keycat address on that network, and should
never receive anything. If a payment of yours ever ends up there, contact
support with the attempt's deposit address.

## Returned payments

When Relay reports a request as refunded and it did not fill, the attempt
becomes `returned`, with Relay's reason, the sender, the amount returned and
the return's transaction. Checkout shows that record to the payer, with a
**Try again** while the charge still takes payments. A request that did fill
is a payment even if Relay also refunded part of it: an overpayment's surplus
is returned on the same request.

## How Keycat learns of a payment

Two independent paths feed one idempotent transition:

1. **Relay's signed notification.** Relay posts each status change with an
   HMAC signature and a timestamp. Keycat accepts a signed notification up to
   an hour old, so Relay's own retries land.
2. **Polling.** The open checkout page reads its attempt, and Keycat queries
   Relay's requests by deposit address, children included, every minute until
   an hour after the attempt's window closes, then less often: every fifteen
   minutes for a day, hourly for a week, then daily.

Either path records the same payment with the same ids, so a missed
notification only delays the update, and neither path can record a payment
twice. A payment is recorded from Relay's destination fill, and the
destination transaction hash on Base is stored with it.

## Settlement and finality

A payment is settled when its USDC transfer on Base is in a block. Base is an
optimistic rollup that posts to Ethereum; a reorganisation of recent Base
blocks is rare, and Keycat reports one as `charge.reversed`. There are no
chargebacks: a transfer to your account cannot be pulled back by the payer or
by Keycat.

## By card

A card payment is the same Relay rail with a fiat onramp in front of it.
Keycat opens an attempt whose origin is USDC on Base, then asks several onramp
providers for exactly the quote's input, delivered as USDC on Base to the
deposit address with no swap. Any provider whose answer would deliver a
different amount, to a different address, or after a conversion is dropped,
because a strict deposit address returns anything else. The payer chooses
among the exact answers; Keycat records the choice from its stored quotes,
never from the browser.

The card attempt's `refundTo` is your business account, because USDC on Base
in and out means a fill Relay cannot make lands where the fill would have.
Keycat records what arrived as a payment: it pays the charge if it covers the
invoice, and leaves it `underpaid` otherwise.

<Callout>
  The onramp provider is the payer's merchant for the card transaction and runs
  its own identity checks. A card dispute is between the payer and that
  provider; the USDC that reached your account stays there.
</Callout>
