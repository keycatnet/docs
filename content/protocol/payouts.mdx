# Payout groups

A payout group shares each payment on a link with other Keycat businesses at
fixed shares. On chain it is a [0xSplits](https://splits.org) v2 **push
split**: a contract that holds the payments sent to it until someone calls
`distribute`, then transfers each recipient's share directly to them.

## Creating a group

Your business account creates the split itself, as an operation your passkey
signs, and pays its gas:

```solidity
PushSplitFactory.createSplitDeterministic(
  params: {
    recipients:            [payee1, payee2, …, yourBusiness],
    allocations:           [shareBps1, shareBps2, …, restBps],
    totalAllocation:       10_000,
    distributionIncentive: 0
  },
  owner:   address(0),
  creator: yourBusiness,
  salt:    keccak256(groupId)
)
```

- **Recipients** are the business accounts of the other Keycat businesses you
  named by handle, then your own, which keeps the rest. Shares are basis
  points that sum to 10,000; a recipient at 0% is left out, because a split
  refuses a zero allocation.
- **`owner = address(0)`** makes the split immutable. Nobody, including you and
  Keycat, can change its recipients or shares. The `creator` is recorded but
  has no power.
- **`distributionIncentive = 0`**: whoever calls `distribute` takes no cut.
- **The salt** is derived from the group's id, so the split's address is a
  function of exactly what you signed. Keycat marks a group ready only after
  reading from Base that a split with those parameters is deployed at that
  address and that its owner is zero.

The same payees at the same shares reuse the existing group, so you never pay
twice for one split. To pay different people, you create a new group.
Archiving a group hides it from new links; links already using it keep paying
it.

## Paying out

A charge that names a group uses the split's address as Relay's recipient, so
each payment lands in the split, not in your account. As soon as the payment
is recorded, Keycat's payouts operator calls `distribute` on that split, and
each recipient, your business included, receives its share in the same
transaction, usually seconds after the customer paid. A backstop pays any
split with a newer payment than its last payout on the next minute, and checks
every split every fifteen minutes.

`distribute` is permissionless. It is checked against the hash of the split's
parameters, so the call must replay the recipients, allocations and incentive
exactly, and it can only pay those recipients those shares. Keycat skips a
payout when the split holds under $0.10 or Base gas is above a cap, and
retries later. Keycat pays the gas for payouts.

A push split keeps a unit or two of USDC dust after each payout, so the split's
balance rarely reads exactly zero. Until a payout runs, your share shows beside
your balance as on its way.

## Refunds of a group payment

The payees were paid when the payment arrived, so a refund of a payment made
through a group comes from your business account alone. Keycat cannot pull a
share back from a payee.
